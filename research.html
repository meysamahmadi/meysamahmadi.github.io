Research

Geotechnical engineering, geophysics, sensing, and data-driven subsurface characterization.

Research Interests

Intelligent Subsurface Sensing

Development of sensing and data-driven approaches for characterizing the subsurface and detecting underground features during construction.

Geotechnical Geophysics

Application of seismic and electrical geophysical methods for non-invasive characterization of subsurface materials and conditions.

Soil Dynamics

Experimental and numerical investigation of the behavior of soils under static, cyclic, and dynamic loading.

Data-Driven Geotechnics

Application of machine learning and statistical methods to integrate geotechnical measurements and multi-physics sensing data.

Current Research

Multi-Physics Intelligent Sensing for Real-Time Look-Ahead While Drilling

My PhD research focuses on developing methods for real-time characterization of the subsurface ahead of drilling operations. The research combines multiple sensing modalities with artificial intelligence to identify subsurface obstructions and improve situational awareness during underground construction.

My work includes developing and evaluating data-driven models, generating and analyzing multi-physics datasets, and studying the performance of sensing approaches under different subsurface conditions.

Measuring While Drilling

I am also interested in the use of measurements collected directly during drilling for geotechnical site characterization. This work includes evaluating existing measuring-while-drilling technologies and investigating how drilling response can provide information about subsurface conditions.

Near-Surface Geophysics

My research interests include seismic and electrical methods for geotechnical characterization, including surface-wave methods, seismic measurements, distributed acoustic sensing, and electrical resistivity tomography.
